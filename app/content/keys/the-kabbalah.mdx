## 1. Defining Kabbalah

<Slide>
  <div>
    ### Core Concept
    The word Kabbalah (Hebrew: Qabbalah) translates to "tradition" or "that which is received"[cite: 13]. In an objective historical context, it refers to the esoteric or "hidden" side of Jewish thought[cite: 14]. 

    Kabbalists treat the Hebrew Bible as a "cryptic text" operating on two distinct levels: **Nigleh** (The Revealed) and **Nistar** (The Concealed)[cite: 17, 18, 19].
  </div>

<Slide>
  <div>
    ### Tradition of Secrecy
    This tradition of secrecy is where we get the English word **Cabal**[cite: 23]. Originally referring to mystical interpretation, the definition shifted by the 1600s to describe an "intriguing society" or a "small group meeting privately"[cite: 24, 25, 26].

    * teachings were historically "concealed by barriers of language"[cite: 21].
    * The "revealed" meaning is often described as a "garment" or "shell" (Kelipah)[cite: 34].

## 2. Denominations

<Slide>
  <div>
    ### The Elite Hierarchy
    Traditionally, Kabbalah was not for the masses[cite: 31]. It was restricted to a "circle of initiates"—scholars over the age of 40 who had already mastered the "Revealed" Torah[cite: 32].

    This ensured that the most "dangerous" or "powerful" information stayed within a small, controlled group[cite: 33].
