<Slide >

    ### Core Concept
    The word Kabbalah (Hebrew: Qabbalah) translates to "tradition" or "that which is received". In an objective historical context, it refers to the esoteric or "hidden" side of Jewish thought[cite: 14]. 

    Kabbalists treat the Hebrew Bible as a "cryptic text" operating on two distinct levels: **Nigleh** (The Revealed) and **Nistar**.

    ### The Elite Hierarchy
    Traditionally, Kabbalah was not for the masses. It was restricted to a "circle of initiates"—scholars over the age of 40 who had already mastered the "Revealed" Torah[cite: 32].

    This ensured that the most "dangerous" or "powerful" information stayed within a small, controlled group[cite: 33].

  <div>
    ### Tradition as a “Received” Text
    The word Kabbalah is often translated as “tradition” (that which is received). In an objective historical framing, it points to the esoteric side of Jewish thought: meanings intended for a small circle rather than the general public.

    Esoteric here means “inner,” while occult here means “hidden forces or spiritual machinery” that the average person cannot directly see.
  </div>
