## 1. The Power of the Keys (The Zohar)

<Slide>
  <div>
    ### Lock and Key
    The PDF frames Kabbalah as more than study: it is described as a “spiritual engine.”

    In that framing, the Bible is treated like a locked box:
    - the <b>Lock</b> is the public, literal text
    - the <b>Key</b> is the Zohar, presented as a decoded set of instructions about how the universe is arranged
  </div>

## 2. The Cabal and Political Power

<Slide>
  <div>
    ### From Kabbalah to “Cabal”
    The PDF connects language and historical influence by noting that the word <b>Cabal</b> is linked to Kabbalah.

    The argument is that when esoteric knowledge is restricted to a tiny elite, it can later be reinterpreted as evidence of secret groups shaping policy and power.

## 3. The Great Debate

<Slide>
  <div>
    ### Divine Light vs. “Luciferian” Criticism
    The PDF summarizes a controversy: practices and texts centered on “hidden forces” can be framed internally as gathering “divine sparks” to repair the world, while external critics interpret the same emphasis as crossing into Luciferian territory.

    It also outlines the split between:
    - internal view (repair, sparks, divine restoration)
    - external view (the “other side,” spirit-commanding, and occult framing)
  </div>
